# SOP Lifecycle Guide

## 1. Identify the need

Use an SOP for a repeatable process that benefits from consistent
instructions, ownership, or clearer handoffs. A policy usually explains
rules or principles. An SOP explains a process. A work instruction
usually explains a narrower task in more detail.

## 2. Assign ownership

Identify a process owner who understands the work and can coordinate
updates. Role-based ownership is often easier to maintain than relying
only on a person's name.

## 3. Understand the process

Identify inputs, outputs, roles, decisions, exceptions, systems,
records, and escalation paths. Compare intended practice with actual
practice.

## 4. Draft

Choose the master or short-form template. Put actions in order, identify
responsibility, and separate normal steps from exceptions.

## 5. Review

Ask appropriate people to review operational accuracy. Obtain specialist
review where your organization or circumstances require it.

## 6. Approve

Use your organization's actual approval authority. Record the approved
version and effective date.

## 7. Implement

Publish the controlled version, communicate changes, and provide
training or transition support where needed.

## 8. Maintain

Update the SOP when the underlying process materially changes.
Periodically verify links, systems, roles, forms, and instructions.

## 9. Retire

When an SOP is replaced or no longer needed, identify it as retired,
address active references, and handle historical copies under your
organization's retention requirements.
